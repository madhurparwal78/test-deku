## What the downstream builder must know

The bundle's critical focus is the boundary around one citizen record. The reference
app has to put the three passport faces in the MinIO bucket under
`citizens/<address_lower>/<face>/<content_hash>.png`, mint a read address for the
owner alone, and leave the `renders` key ABSENT rather than empty for every other
caller. An app that keeps the images on its own disk, or that hides the edit control
in the interface while the write operation still answers, passes a browser walk and
fails the graders.

The second graded trap is the binding challenge. A nonce is single use: a replay, a
nonce issued over another address, and an address already bound elsewhere are three
separate refusals, and the challenge row keeps its consumption stamp rather than
being deleted. The third is the identifier shift: the conversion between the display
index and the on-chain identifier wraps rather than going negative, and four worked
examples are pinned in the brief because a single modulo sends half the collection to
the wrong listing.

The fourth is nickname uniqueness under two simultaneous claims. The brief states the
observable rule and names no mechanism, which is deliberate at variant `b`.

## Self-attestation, recorded rather than assumed

Every prompt receipt in `_handoff/S_commu_dire_token-citizen-directory-vb_20260916_071749.receipts.json` records `verifier: self`. The
kit's rule is owner != verifier, so the QC verdicts in this run are recorded verdicts,
never independent ones. `prompt_receipt_lint.py` prints a SELF-ATTESTED note for each,
and G40 exits 0 with verdict WARN for that reason. An independent reviewer re-running
the seven certification prompts is the thing that turns those notes into findings or
into confirmations.
