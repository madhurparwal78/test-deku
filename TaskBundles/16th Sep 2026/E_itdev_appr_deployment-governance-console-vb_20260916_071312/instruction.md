# Meridian Deployment Governance Console

Build and deploy a working web application from this brief. There is no starting codebase. When you are done, a stranger must be able to open the app in a browser and take a built deployment of the `checkout-web` project from its preview address all the way to production: raise a promotion request against the production environment, watch two eligible reviewers approve it, see traffic move to that deployment, and read every one of those transitions back out of the audit log with the actor, the principal type, the request identifier and the before and after state, without hitting an error page. A different stranger signed in as `developer@example.com` must NOT be able to approve their own promotion request by any means, including a direct API call with a valid token: the request must still show zero approvals afterwards and the production alias must still point where it did. The promotion must become real in the database, not just on screen; a green tick the app returns to itself does not count, and neither does an approval count that moves in the browser while the stored request is untouched.

## Overview

Meridian is an enterprise control plane for organisations that ship web applications and AI agents. It has two surfaces that share one identity system and one design system. The public surface is a marketing, pricing, security, documentation, template and editorial site that anyone can read. The governed console behind that identity system holds teams, projects, deployments, access groups, spend limits, firewall rules, observability and audit logs. The two are one product: the public surface is not a brochure bolted onto a separate application, it shares the header, the type scale and the identity layer, and its central job is to move a visitor through sign-up into the console.

The people who use it are a visitor comparing plans and reading documentation, a developer who connects a repository and opens a preview, a reviewer who approves or rejects a promotion, a team admin who invites people and assigns roles, an organisation owner who sets policy and reads audit, a finance admin who sets budgets and exports cost attribution, a security reviewer who writes firewall rules, and an agent principal that deploys under a short-lived credential. A principal is anything that can act: an account, an agent, or a token. An agent is a first-class member with its own role, its own scope, its own owner and its own expiry, and its authority is bounded by the live authority of the account that created it, recomputed on every request rather than frozen at creation.

One workflow defines the build. A developer pushes a commit. A build is queued, runs, and produces a deployment in `READY` state on a preview address. A promotion request is opened against the production environment. The policy engine finds that the target environment requires two approvals from a named access group, and that the requester is not eligible to approve their own request. Two reviewers approve. The promotion executes, traffic shifts, an audit record is written for every one of those transitions with actor, principal type, address, request identifier and before and after state, and a webhook fires to the customer's own systems exactly once. Every other feature here exists to make that sentence true under concurrency, partial failure and retry.

The genuinely hard part is that promotion is a race and an authorization boundary at the same time: two approvals arriving in the same instant must both be recorded, two promotions of different deployments to the same alias must produce exactly one winner and one explicit conflict, and a principal who is not eligible to approve must be refused by the server even when the interface would never have shown them the control.

This is a control plane, not the execution substrate. It does not run customer builds, serve customer traffic, or terminate TLS. It records the deployment, owns the state machine, enforces the policy, and writes the history. It deliberately does not build: real container execution, real edge points of presence, real DNS or certificate issuance, a second region, a payment card form, an email or chat channel, a search engine over customer logs, or any outbound call to a third-party network.

## User roles

Four seeded principals across two teams. Signup is closed: accounts are seeded and no self-service registration endpoint exists. Permissions are scoped by relationship as well as by role - a principal sees only the teams they are a member of, and a reviewer approves only requests raised inside a team they belong to.

| Role | Can read | Can write |
|---|---|---|
| `developer` | projects, deployments, builds, logs and the approval queue of their own teams | connect a repository, trigger a build, open a promotion request, cancel their own request. **Cannot approve any promotion request, including their own. Cannot change a firewall rule, a spend cap, a role or a membership. Cannot read another team's anything.** |
| `reviewer` | everything a `developer` reads, plus the whole approval queue and the audit log of their own teams | approve or reject a promotion request raised by someone else, manage firewall rules, run break-glass. **Cannot open a promotion request for a deployment they built and then approve it. Cannot change billing, a spend cap or an invoice. Cannot deploy.** |
| `finance` | usage, spend policy, invoices and cost attribution of their own teams | set a budget, set a spend cap, acknowledge an alert, export cost attribution. **Cannot deploy, cannot promote, cannot approve, cannot read source, cannot read runtime logs, cannot read another team's usage.** |
| `viewer` | project, deployment list and deployment status of their own teams | nothing at all. **Cannot read environment values, log lines, invoice amounts or member email addresses.** |

Authorization is enforced **server-side on every mutating endpoint**. Hiding a button in the UI is not authorization: a direct API call from a `developer` session to any `reviewer`-only endpoint must be rejected by the server (an unauthorized request is denied, not served), leaving the protected state unchanged.

Three rules go further than role alone, and each is checkable. An explicit deny outranks every allow at every scope, so one project can be taken away from a group without dismantling the group. A refusal for a resource that exists but is invisible and a refusal for a resource that does not exist return the same shape and the same class of response, so the API cannot be used to discover another team's project names. And a bulk operation evaluates permission per item and returns a per-item result, never one decision for the whole set.

## Core features

### Auth

Accounts are seeded, signup is closed, and the app owns the session. `POST /api/auth/login` takes `{"email", "password"}` and returns `{"access_token": "..."}`. Every other endpoint except `GET /api/health`, the public marketing reads and the webhook receiver requires `Authorization: Bearer <token>`. Passwords are stored hashed, never in clear. A token expires eight hours after it is issued and an expired token is refused. The identity provider, Keycloak, is the directory of record for accounts and group membership, reachable at `AUTH_ISSUER_URL` with the client credentials in `AUTH_CLIENT_ID` and `AUTH_CLIENT_SECRET`; the app exchanges against it and mints its own bearer token, so the login endpoint above is the only sign-in shape this product exposes.

1. A login with a wrong password is rejected as invalid, and the response is identical in shape to a login for an address that has no account. Neither response ever says which of the two happened.
2. No state-changing operation is reachable by `GET`. Every mutation is a `POST`, `PATCH`, `PUT` or `DELETE`.
3. The log-in screen offers, in this order: an email field with `Continue with Email`, then a rule, then `Continue with Google`, `Continue with GitHub`, `Continue with ChatGPT`, `Continue with SAML SSO`, `Continue with Passkey`, then `Show other options`, then a line reading `Don't have an account?` with a `Sign Up` link, then `Terms` and `Privacy Policy`. The sign-up screen offers `Google`, `GitHub`, `ChatGPT`, `Apple`, then `Show other options`, then `Continue with Email` as a text control with a trailing arrow. `SAML SSO` is absent from sign up: enterprise identity is joined by invitation or by domain claim, never by self-service. Only the email path is wired to a working session in this build; every other provider control renders, is reachable by keyboard, and reports that the connection is not configured for this organisation.
4. Both identity screens fit one viewport at every supported size and never grow a scrollbar.
5. Cancelling a provider sign-in returns to the same screen with no error shown, because cancelling is not a failure.
6. **Multi-factor.** An organisation may require a second factor, and when it does every member must satisfy one before any request other than enrolment succeeds. The factors are a passkey, time-based codes from an authenticator, and single-use recovery codes that are shown once, stored hashed, and raise a notification when one is used. A passkey used as the primary factor already satisfies the requirement, because it is two factors on its own. The satisfied time is held on the session and is required again, recently, for a step-up action.
7. **Step-up.** Approving a promotion, transferring ownership, changing the directory connection, disabling the second-factor requirement, rotating an encryption key, deleting a project, changing a spend cap and exporting audit data all require a second factor satisfied within the last fifteen minutes regardless of how old the session is. An unsatisfied obligation is a denial, not a warning.
8. **Anti-abuse and enumeration.** The response to an email submission is identical in shape whether or not an account exists, and its timing is equalised by a fixed floor, because a faster refusal is how somebody works out who has an account. Rate limits apply per address, per network and per account with the strictest winning. A failed attempt never reveals which factor failed. An account lock names the reason class and the unlock path and never shows a countdown that leaks the attempt count. An invitation is bound to the address it was sent to, and signing in with a different address shows the mismatch and offers to switch account.
9. **Session mechanics.** A session is an opaque server-side record and the cookie carries a random identifier and nothing else - no claim about the principal ever travels in it. The cookie is set `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Lax`, host-only and at path `/`; `Lax` rather than `Strict`, because the return from an identity provider is a cross-site top-level navigation that `Strict` would break. Idle expiry is eight hours and absolute expiry is thirty days, and an organisation may shorten both but never lengthen them. Signing in, satisfying a second factor and accepting an invitation each mint a new session identifier and invalidate the old one. `/account` lists every active session with its address, coarse location, device class and last-seen time, each individually revocable, beside a revoke-all-others control.

### Teams, projects and the address structure

A team is addressed by its slug at the first path segment, so `/northwind/checkout-web` is the `checkout-web` project inside the `northwind` team. Team-level sections sit under a `~` segment, which can never be a team slug or a project slug: `/northwind/~/members`, `/northwind/~/access-groups`, `/northwind/~/audit`, `/northwind/~/billing`, `/northwind/~/settings`. Project sections sit below the project: `/northwind/checkout-web/deployments`, `/northwind/checkout-web/approvals`, `/northwind/checkout-web/firewall`, `/northwind/checkout-web/observability`, `/northwind/checkout-web/logs`, `/northwind/checkout-web/settings`.

1. A team slug that matches any reserved public route is refused as invalid and no team row is written. The reserved list is `login`, `signup`, `docs`, `pricing`, `blog`, `changelog`, `customers`, `templates`, `security`, `enterprise`, `ai`, `i`, `new`, `account`, `teams`, `api`, `v1`, `sso`, `verify`, `mfa`, `passkey`, `notifications`, `search`, `privacy`, `terms`, `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt`.
2. Two teams may not share a slug and two projects inside one team may not share a slug. A second attempt at a taken slug is refused and leaves the first untouched.
3. Switching teams is a navigation to the new team's address, never a swap of client state, so a response for the previous team cannot arrive late and paint another team's data under this team's heading.
4. The team switcher names the current team, lists every team the principal belongs to with its plan as a chip, lists the personal scope first, and offers a create-team action.
5. The breadcrumb is the address made legible: team, then project, then section. Each segment past the first opens a menu of its siblings, so the project segment lists the other projects in the team.

### Deployments and the build

A deployment is an immutable build artefact plus its routing state. Being live is a property of an alias, not of a deployment, which is what makes rollback a re-point rather than a rebuild.

1. The deployment states are exactly `QUEUED`, `BUILDING`, `READY`, `ERROR`, `CANCELED`, `PROMOTING`, `LIVE` and `SUPERSEDED`, in that casing. `READY` means built and reachable on its own preview address. `LIVE` is carried by the alias.
2. Every state transition is written as an event as well as projected onto the deployment row, in the same transaction, so the history is never lost and the current state is a single indexed read.
   Triggering a deployment happens from exactly four sources, and each records a different principal. A `git` push arriving on a watched branch is attributed to the commit author's linked account where one exists. A `cli` upload is attributed to the account or agent behind the token. An `api` create call is attributed to the token's principal. An `agent` trigger is attributed to the agent, with its creating account recorded beside it.
3. `POST /api/webhooks/git` receives a push. It verifies the `X-Meridian-Signature` header against the raw body before parsing anything, refuses a `X-Meridian-Timestamp` more than five minutes old, records the delivery identifier, and answers within three seconds by enqueuing rather than by building inline.
4. **The same delivery identifier, or the same project, source kind and commit together, produces exactly one deployment.** A webhook delivered twice must not create a second deployment and must not start a second build; the second delivery is answered successfully and changes nothing. Git providers retry aggressively, and a duplicate build is both a cost and a race for the alias.
5. A build that fails moves the deployment to `ERROR` and **leaves the live alias exactly where it was**. Nothing about a failed build ever touches production.
6. Build logs stream over a resumable connection keyed by an offset, so a reconnect resumes rather than restarts. Environment values are injected at provision time and never echoed into a log line: log output is scrubbed against the set of secret values before it is stored, not when it is rendered.
7. Build concurrency is limited per team by plan, and a queued deployment shows its real position in the queue, not an estimate.
8. The deployment list is keyset-paginated newest first on creation time and identifier together. Offset paging is not offered on any list that can grow past a hundred thousand rows.

### Promotion, the approval chain and the critical rule

Promotion is the one state-changing action this product is arranged around, and it is a workflow rather than a button.

An approval policy is versioned and attaches to an environment. It carries a required approval count, the eligible access groups, a self-approval flag defaulting to false, a distinct-approver flag defaulting to true, an expiry in minutes, and optional freeze windows. The seeded production policy for `checkout-web` is named `Production two-eyes`, is version `1`, requires `2` approvals from the `release-reviewers` access group, forbids self-approval, requires distinct approvers and expires an open request after `1440` minutes.

A promotion request carries a state from exactly `OPEN`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED`, `CANCELED`, `EXECUTED` and `FAILED`, plus the deployment, the requester, the policy version it opened under, the required approval count and a typed justification.

1. **A promotion request may be approved only by a principal holding the `reviewer` role who is a member of an eligible access group at the moment of approving, and who is not the requester.** A `developer` calling the approve endpoint with a valid token is denied by the server, **and the underlying request row must not change**: its approval count stays as it was, its state stays `OPEN`, and no approval decision is recorded. The same holds for `finance` and for `viewer`, and it holds for a direct API call exactly as it holds in the browser.
2. The requester may not approve their own request unless self-approval is enabled. It is disabled on the seeded policy, so `developer@example.com` approving their own request is refused and the row is untouched.
3. Two approvals must come from two distinct principals. The same reviewer approving twice is refused as a duplicate and the second attempt records nothing.
4. Eligibility is evaluated fresh at the moment of approving, never at the moment the request opened. A principal removed from the eligible access group this morning cannot approve this afternoon.
5. One rejection closes the request into `REJECTED` with a mandatory comment. A rejection is a stop, not a vote to be outweighed, and reopening requires a new request.
6. **Two approvals arriving at the same instant are both recorded.** The request row carries a version and every transition writes with that version in its predicate, so a lost update returns a conflict the caller can refetch rather than silently dropping one of the two approvals. Losing an approval in a two-approval policy means a change reaching production with one.
7. A policy change never retroactively alters an open request. The request executes under the policy version it recorded.
8. Approval and execution are separate steps. Reaching the approval count writes `APPROVED`; a worker then executes the promotion and writes `EXECUTED`. A failed execution writes `FAILED` with its reason and **keeps the approvals**, so a retry does not go round the chain again. If the deployment changed state in between, execution fails with that specific reason and a new request is required.
9. **Two promotions racing for the same alias produce exactly one winner.** The loser is refused with a conflict that names the winning deployment, and is never silently dropped. The alias is re-pointed, its previous deployment identifier is recorded, the superseded deployment moves to `SUPERSEDED`, an event is written and an outbox row is written, all in one transaction.
10. A freeze window blocks execution, not approval, so a change can be approved during a freeze and executed when it lifts.
11. Rollback is one control on a superseded deployment: promote it again. Same authorization, same audit record, same alias re-point, and the same elapsed time regardless of the size of the deployment or how old it is.
12. The approval surface at `/northwind/checkout-web/approvals` is a board of open requests grouped by state, newest first, each card showing the deployment, the commit, the requester, the elapsed time, the approvals gathered against the approvals required and the time left before expiry. Opening one shows the commit, the author, the message, the changed paths, the environment-value diff **by key with values never rendered**, and the preview address. The gathered count updates live so two reviewers looking at the same request see each other's decision without reloading. A principal sees only the requests they are eligible to act on plus their own.

### Audit log

`See exactly what happened: every action, every tool call, who initiated it, and what it returned is inspectable in Audit Logs.` That sentence is the standard this feature is built to.

1. Every state change, and every read of a sensitive resource, writes one audit record carrying a gapless per-organisation sequence number, the time, the actor principal and its kind, the account an agent acted on behalf of, a stable verb from a closed catalogue, the resource kind and identifier, the resource label as it was at the time, the outcome `allow`, `deny` or `error`, the authorization reason on a denial, a redacted before and after, a summary of what the action returned, the request identifier, the address and the policy versions that applied.
2. **The audit log is append-only at the privilege level.** No role the application holds may update or delete an audit row, and the app asserts that at startup and refuses to serve if the grant is wrong.
3. Each record carries a hash over its own content plus the previous record's hash, forming a chain per organisation. A daily verification walks the previous day's chain and publishes a checkpoint holding the head hash, the sequence number and a timestamp. A verification failure is an incident, not a log line.
4. Agent actions carry the principal kind `agent` and the creating account, and a tool call made by an agent is its own record with the scopes requested and the outcome.
5. The verb catalogue covers at minimum: authentication including failures, session creation and revocation, membership and role changes, access group and grant changes, promotion requests and decisions, deployment creation and promotion, alias changes, environment variable creation, update and read, firewall rule changes, spend policy changes and pauses, integration installation and removal, credential mints, audit exports, break-glass start and end, directory synchronisation and key rotations.
6. An audit record is never deleted, by anyone, at any level of access, and an account is never hard deleted either: it is suspended and then anonymised, and the audit rows keep the opaque principal identifier so the chain stays intact.
7. Break-glass is an emergency elevation for a `reviewer`. It is always time-bounded with a default of sixty minutes and a ceiling of four hours, always demands a typed justification and a ticket reference, always notifies every organisation owner at the moment it starts rather than in a digest, and always writes a distinctly typed audit record.
8. **Tamper evidence is the checkpoint, not the chain.** A chain of hashes alone proves nothing against somebody who can write to the store, because they recompute every hash. What makes it real evidence is that the daily checkpoint is also written to append-only external storage under a separate credential, so an attacker holding the database cannot rewrite history and recompute the chain undetected.
9. **Residency.** An organisation declares a data region and a residency mode of `flexible` or `pinned`. Under `pinned`, its control-plane records, its telemetry, its logs and its backups are held only in the declared region, and **a cross-region read is refused rather than proxied**. Availability never overrides residency: a region in trouble fails within its region rather than failing over across a boundary, because the alternative is a compliance breach performed automatically during an incident. The setting is fixed after creation except through a supervised migration, because a residency change is a movement of data and not a flag.
10. **Retention and erasure.** A retention policy carries a separate window for audit, telemetry, logs and deployments. It is configurable downward from the plan maximum and upward only to that maximum, with a floor imposed by whichever compliance regime the organisation has declared, and **a retention sweep that would delete below the floor fails loudly rather than proceeding**. A data-subject request is one of access, erasure, rectification, portability or restriction, its identity is verified before anything is disclosed, and erasure anonymises rather than deletes. Erasure covers every derived store, not only the main one: the relational records, the telemetry, the logs, the caches, the search index and the exports. **A maintained inventory of the stores data spreads to is what makes the promise keepable**; an erasure that misses the search index is not an erasure. Every request and every step of it is itself audited.
11. **Compliance evidence is generated from the records the product already runs on**, never maintained as a separate document, because a separately maintained document is wrong by the time it is read. The regimes the platform claims are `SOC 2 Type 2`, `PCI DSS`, `ISO 27001`, `EU-U.S. DPF`, `HIPAA BAA` and `TISAX`, and each is evidenced from what is already here: access reviews from the grants plus the audit log, change management from the approval workflow, encryption attestation from the key metadata, availability from the service objectives, and incident records from the firewall and platform incidents. An access review is a scheduled export of every grant, its subject, its scope, its source and its expiry, delivered to organisation owners quarterly with an acknowledgement recorded.
12. **Encryption and key management.** Values are wrapped by an organisation-scoped key held in a managed key service, and a plaintext data key exists only in memory. Field-level encryption covers environment variable values, provider refresh tokens, directory bearer tokens and webhook secrets. An organisation may hold its own key, and the consequence is stated plainly at configuration time and requires typed confirmation: **revoking that key renders their data permanently unreadable, backups included, and that is the intended behaviour**.

### Firewall

1. A firewall rule carries a name, ordered condition groups, one action from `allow`, `deny`, `challenge`, `log`, `rate-limit` or `bypass`, a priority, an enabled flag and an environment scope. Conditions read the request path, method, host, header, cookie presence, query parameter, source address, address range, country, autonomous system number, user-agent class, bot score and rate counters.
2. Rules evaluate by priority ascending, first match wins, with `bypass` evaluated before everything so an operator can always let themselves in. Managed rulesets evaluate after the system protections and before custom rules.
3. The three seeded rules are exactly `Log request starting with /`, `Challenge user agents that look like bots` and `Deny traffic from Germany`.
4. **Plan limits are data read from the same plan definition the pricing page renders**, so the advertised cap and the enforced cap are one number: custom rules `3` on Hobby, `40` on Pro and `1000` on Enterprise; address blocks `3`, `100` and `1000`; system bypass rules `0`, `25` and `100`; the managed core ruleset on Enterprise only. Reaching a limit disables the create control with the limit named and an upgrade path, before the rule is written, never as a failure at save time.
5. The rule editor carries a preview that counts how many requests in the last hour the rule **would have** matched, before the rule is enabled. Writing a deny rule against live traffic without knowing its blast radius is how a firewall takes a site down.
6. A challenge is a degraded mode, not a default. While it is active the console says so prominently with the reason and an off control, and issuance, pass and fail counts are recorded per rule so an operator can see a challenge that is turning away real visitors. The invisible bot signal, BotSignal, runs before any visible challenge, and a visible challenge appears only when that signal is inconclusive and a rule demands it.
7. An incident opens automatically on a sustained anomaly, notifies immediately, resolves after a quiet period, and is never deleted. It records the peak requests per second, the total requests, the top rules and the top sources.
8. A rule change propagates as a versioned bundle carrying a monotonic version and a checksum. The console shows the propagation state as a fraction of locations already serving the current version, and **a rule is not described as active until that fraction is complete**. A bundle that fails its checksum is rejected and the previous version keeps serving.

### Spend management

1. Money is integer minor units in `usd` throughout, in the store, in the API, in the interface and in every export. **No floating-point value touches a monetary amount anywhere**, because a fraction of a cent becomes a support queue. Pro is `2000` minor units a month, Hobby is `0`, and Enterprise is quoted.
2. Every usage record carries an idempotency key derived from the emitting system and its own window, and ingestion upserts on that key. **A metering retry must not charge twice.**
3. Rollups are recomputed from the underlying usage rather than accumulated in place, and each records the price version it used, so a period can be reproduced exactly months later. Usage arriving late lands in the open period for a grace window and afterwards lands in the next period, tagged with its original window so an invoice stays explainable.
4. `Set a limit, get alerts, and we'll stop usage before you go over budget.` is a promise to stop, not to warn. Soft thresholds notify once each per period. At the hard cap the configured action runs: `notify` warns only, `restrict` allows a reduced number of new builds each hour and refuses the rest with the reason, and `pause` stops new builds and non-production deployments **while production traffic keeps being served**, because taking a customer's live site down to save them money is the worse outcome. A pause writes an audit record, notifies at once, and is reversible by an authorised principal in one action.
5. The usage figure the cap is compared against is never more than five minutes behind, and the interface states that lag on screen beside it.
6. An invoice is annotated, never edited. A correction is a separate credit note.
7. Every usage record carries its full scope chain, so an invoice breaks down by team and by project without re-deriving anything, and a cost-attribution export is a signed, expiring download whose generation is itself audited.
8. **Commerce state is a projection, never a local assumption.** A subscription carries its plan, its status, its current period, whether it cancels at period end, a trial end and a seat count. A checkout completion is idempotent on its session identifier, so **a double completion - a retried notification and a browser return arriving at the same moment - produces exactly one subscription**, never two. Commerce notifications arrive out of order and more than once: each is deduplicated by its own event identifier through an inbox with a uniqueness rule, each handler compares the event's sequence against what is already stored and discards a stale one, and every subscription change writes an audit record.
9. A failed payment enters a defined sequence of notices with a defined grace period before any restriction, and **a restriction never includes taking production traffic offline**. A trial ending notifies at seven days, one day and on the day, each naming the consequence. A plan downgraded below current usage is accepted, and the resources over the new limit are named with a deadline rather than being deleted.

### Notifications, integrations and webhooks

1. Recipients are resolved from roles and access groups at the moment the event happens, never from a stored list of addresses, so a principal removed from a group stops receiving that group's notifications in the same instant.
2. A small set of events bypasses every digest and every mute because their whole value is immediacy: a break-glass grant starting, a spend hard cap reached and any resulting pause, a directory connection changed or a certificate within seven days of expiry, an audit chain verification failure, a high-severity firewall incident opened, and a payment failure whose grace period is ending. The preference screen says these cannot be switched off rather than showing a switch that does nothing.
3. A notification waiting to be sent to a principal who has just lost access to its scope is dropped before sending, not after.
4. Outbound webhooks are signed over the raw body plus a timestamp, support two concurrent secrets so rotation needs no downtime, and are delivered **at least once** with a stable event identifier so the receiver can deduplicate. Exactly-once delivery over a network is not offered and is not claimed. Retries back off exponentially with jitter across roughly a day, then the endpoint is disabled and its owner is notified. Delivery attempts are visible with the response status and an excerpt, and a single delivery is replayable by an authorised principal. A destination is checked against internal address ranges on every attempt, including after a redirect.
5. Every event is published from an outbox row written in the same transaction as the state change it describes, and a relay sends it afterwards. **No event is ever published directly from a handler**, because a sent message about a rolled-back transaction cannot be taken back.
6. The event catalogue covers `deployment.created`, `deployment.succeeded`, `deployment.failed`, `deployment.canceled`, `deployment.promoted`, `rollout.advanced`, `rollout.halted`, `promotion.requested`, `promotion.approved`, `promotion.rejected`, `promotion.executed`, `domain.verified`, `firewall.incident.opened`, `firewall.incident.resolved`, `budget.threshold.reached`, `budget.cap.reached`, `budget.paused`, `member.added`, `member.removed`, `role.changed`, `grant.expired` and `audit.export.completed`.
7. A credential broker, Connect, holds long-lived provider credentials and hands a principal a short-lived token carrying a subset of the grant's scopes. The long-lived credential never leaves the platform, every mint is audited with the scopes requested and the scopes granted, minted tokens default to fifteen minutes, and revoking a grant revokes every token it minted. A marketplace integration installs into a team behind a screen naming exactly what it will be able to read and do, and its own access is subject to the same decision every other principal's is; an integration is not a bypass.

### Observability

1. A restricted, structured query surface serves the traffic data. A query names an aggregate over a numeric field or a count, a source from `requests`, `functions`, `firewall` or `builds`, a filter, a grouping, a field to visualize, an order, a time range and a limit. The aggregates are `AVG`, `SUM`, `MAX`, `MIN`, `count` and the percentiles `p50`, `p75`, `p90`, `p95` and `p99`. The measured examples the marketing page floats are all expressible: `WHERE cache='hit'`, `GROUP BY source_path`, `VISUALIZE bandwidth_incoming` and `WHERE status >= 500`.
2. The query is parsed into a typed tree and compiled. **The scope filter is appended by the compiler and cannot be expressed, overridden or removed by the query text**, so a query is scoped whether or not its author remembered.
3. Every query carries a cost estimate and a row limit, and one that exceeds its estimate is refused before it runs, with the estimate shown and a narrower range suggested.
4. A saved query is an object with its own permissions and **executes as the reader, never as its author**, so saving a query is not a way of lending out access.
5. A telemetry gap renders as a gap with its extent marked, never as a zero. A zero on a traffic chart reads as an outage and starts a false incident.
6. The log viewer stays smooth however long the log gets, and holds a stable anchor: following the tail pins to the bottom, scrolling up unpins, and a control returns to the tail. A log line carries a timestamp, a level, a deployment, a region, a request identifier, a message and structured fields, and the request identifier links across to the matching telemetry row and trace.
7. No customer-supplied value becomes a grouping dimension without passing an allowlist and a cardinality budget, and a dimension that exceeds its budget collapses into an overflow bucket named as such rather than being dropped in silence.
8. An alert holds a query, a comparison, a threshold, a duration the condition must hold before it fires, a severity and its channels. It deduplicates per scope and resolves explicitly, so a single spike never pages anyone.
9. **Tracing.** Every request carries a trace identifier propagated in the standard trace-context header, spanning the control plane, the build record and the edge configuration. The request identifier is surfaced in the interface, in log lines, in error responses and in support references, so a customer can quote one string and be found. Trace sampling is decided at the head at a configured rate, with a tail rule that always keeps a trace containing an error or exceeding a latency threshold.
10. **Search.** One search surface serves documentation on the public routes and teams, projects, deployments and settings inside the console. **Ranking is field-weighted with recency as a tiebreaker**, and an exact title match outranks a body match. Every query is scope-filtered exactly as every other read is, so the index can never return a result the reader may not open. The result count is announced politely as it changes.
11. **The documentation assistant.** The `Ask AI` action opens an assistant over the documentation. Its retrieval is scope-filtered exactly as search is: **an assistant answering from a document the reader could not open is the same leak as returning that document**. Its answer streams into a region established before the streaming starts, it cites the documents it drew on, and it is never the only route to an answer - every document it quotes is reachable by navigation as well.

**Entry and redirects.** An unauthenticated request for any console route goes to `/login` carrying the requested path as a return target; the target is validated against an allowlist of internal paths and is never a full address. After a successful login the principal lands on that return target, or on `/northwind` when there is none. `Visit Your Firewall` on the security page is the one public control that leaves for the console: signed in it resolves to the principal's default team firewall, signed out it routes through `/login` with the return target rather than dropping them on a console error. Logging out clears the session and returns to `/`. A token that expires mid-action returns the principal to `/login` with the return target intact, and the shell keeps whatever was typed in the open form so it is restored afterwards. A principal who reaches a route their role does not entitle them to sees the same refusal a missing resource produces, never a hint that the resource exists. A permission changed mid-session is reflected on the next navigation, because no permission decision is cached across one.

Every page has a loading state sized to the content that is coming, so nothing jumps when it arrives. ## UI/UX notes

The north star is comprehension: somebody opening this should understand, in the first moment, what state their production is in and what is waiting on them. This is an operational product, a control plane read and scanned many times a day, so the register is quiet, dense but organised, built for repeated action. It is not an editorial composition and it carries no oversized hero inside the console. The public surface is allowed more air than the console, and even there the working miniatures are the subject rather than decoration around it. Clarity over atmosphere, and density over generous spacing wherever a full queue on one screen is worth more than white space.

**Type.** The interface is set in `Geist`, with `Inter` and then the platform sans stack behind it, and code and metrics are set in `Geist Mono` with the platform monospace stack behind it. Both families are variable across weight `100` to `900`. The sans face is loaded so that it swaps while it arrives and the mono face is loaded so that it blocks, which is deliberate: swapping a body face is a reflow the reader forgives, and swapping a monospace face inside a code block reflows the whole block. The rendered scale is `16px` at weight 400 with `24px` leading for body and the default for everything, `14px` at 400 with `20px` leading for secondary body, table cells and captions, `20px` at 400 with `32px` leading for a lead paragraph, `18px` at 400 with `28px` leading for documentation intro, `14px` at 500 with `20px` leading for control labels and button text, `13px` at 400 with `20px` leading for metadata, timestamps and badges, `16px` at 500 for emphasised body and the active navigation item, `22px` at 400 with `33px` leading for a small section heading, `11px` at 500 uppercase for an eyebrow label, `12px` at 500 with `16px` leading for a badge or chip, and then the headings, of which `24px` is the third tier, `32px` the second and `56px` and `64px` the display and hero tiers, all at weight `450`, with the last two setting their leading equal to their size so the hero sets tight on two lines. **Weight `450` is the signature of this system and is not a rounding of `400` or `500`.** Ship a variable face and set headings at `450`; a static face rounded to `500` renders every heading heavier than it should be. Figures align wherever amounts stack, so tabular numerals are on in tables, in the totals strip and in every money column. Tracking tightens slightly as the size grows and never on body copy.

**Colour, by role.** Twelve neutrals carry the whole product and colour arrives only to say something. The page ground is a near-white neutral, with a second slightly darker near-white neutral for inset grounds, code blocks and table stripes. Borders are a near-white neutral one step down, dividers one step darker again, placeholder text a mid neutral, secondary text a darker mid neutral, and primary text a near-black neutral which is also the ground of the primary button. The accent is a mid, vivid blue, it is the focus colour, it is the link colour, and **it is the only thing on a page wearing it**: a second blue element competing with the focused control is the failure to avoid. Status carries its own meanings and each appears nowhere else: a mid, vivid red for a failure or a rejection, a green for a success or a healthy build, an amber for a warning or a pending state, and, for the legacy accent set the console still uses on its state chips, a mid, vivid indigo, a light, vivid magenta, a light, vivid teal, a mid, vivid orange and a mid, vivid violet. The exact shades are yours, so long as each meaning-carrying colour is used for that meaning and for nothing else, and so long as the neutral ramp stays a ramp rather than a set of unrelated greys. Do not build a page dominated by one hue family with no second signal, and never let decoration stand in for content.

**Contrast and the focus ring are contract, not taste.** Body text meets WCAG AA at four and a half to one against its ground; large text and interface components meet three to one. The focus ring is two stops - a hairline of page ground, then the accent outside it - and the inner stop is the part people delete to save effort. Delete it and the ring vanishes the moment a focused control sits on the dark primary button, and someone using keyboard navigation loses their place. It is present on every focusable element, everywhere, in both themes.

**Shape, density and elevation.** Corners are rounded by about the width of a comma, uniformly, on cards, inputs and buttons; chips and pills are fully rounded at their ends instead. Control heights come in three steps, a small, a medium and a large, and every control takes its height from one of those three rather than from its own content, and decoration inside a control is small enough to sit inside the label rather than beside it. Space runs on a four-unit base with a named ladder, so nothing is spaced by eye. Elevation is a hairline border plus the faintest possible shadow rather than a soft glow, which is what makes this product read as precise rather than plump, and the menu, modal and tooltip elevations are composites of that hairline with progressively deeper, still very light, shadows. Rows in the deployment board and the audit list sit tight so a full queue fits one screen.

**Motion.** The character is eased: almost everything that moves does so briskly and settles, on one curve, at one short duration, and that single choice is most of why the product feels like one thing. The four properties that change together on every interactive element are its text colour, its ground, its border and its outline, and they move as one. There is one exception, reserved for things that arrive over the top of the page - the navigation panels, the command menu, dialogs and drawers - which travel a fraction past where they are going and settle back, the way a well-made drawer does, and that overshoot is allowed on movement and fade only, never on a colour, because a colour that overshoots flickers past the shade it was aiming for. The named moments, each of which must exist: a fade in, a fade out, a soft fade in from partly visible, a slide in from below, a fade and slide in from above, a matched show and hide pair driven by a start and end offset the call site supplies so one definition serves a toast rising, a tooltip dropping and a row sliding into a table, a spinner that fades rather than spins, a bar pulse for a queued build, a thinking loader that sweeps its ground for a running build, a blue glow that breathes, a logo carousel that holds each mark visible for about a fifth of its cycle with a short fade at each end, a marquee that translates continuously, a skeleton whose ground sweeps while content is pending, a shimmer, a stroke that draws itself, a caret that blinks in the deployment terminal, and a cursor that floats. The three background graphs on the enterprise page drift at three different speeds, and the ratio between them is the depth; keep them genuinely different or the layers visibly fall back into step. The customer carousel's speed is computed from the number of marks rather than pasted, so adding a mark does not make the row drift. Tooltips in a group wait noticeably before the first one appears and then only briefly for the rest while any is open, which is what lets someone scan a row of icon buttons without a label popping up at every pass. **Under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing loops, nothing enters, and nothing is lost**: carousels become a static row, the marquee becomes a scrollable row with a visible affordance, pulses and glows hold at their resting frame, entrance animations are replaced by their end state, overlays keep their fade and drop their movement, reveals render already revealed, and skeletons render as a flat block.

**Scroll.** This product is not scroll-scrubbed and must not become so: the documentation, editorial, catalogue and console routes change nothing at all as they scroll, because these are surfaces people read, search and link into, and content that assembles itself as you scroll is slower to read and lands you in the wrong place when you follow a link. Where something does appear on approach it is triggered once, by its intersection with the viewport, when its top edge reaches the lower part of it, it does not reverse on the way back up, and **the page is delivered already revealed and hidden a frame later**, so a visitor whose scripting never runs reads a complete page rather than a blank one. The documentation sidebar fades to nothing at its bottom edge rather than being cut off, and the logo row fades at both ends, so marks dissolve at the edges instead of vanishing at a hard line. A long document carries a soft fade pinned to the bottom of the viewport so content never butts hard against the edge.

**Responsive.** The layout holds at every width between a small phone, a large phone, a tablet, a laptop and the page grid maximum, and those five widths are the only real breakpoints - every other width condition in the product is local to one component - and it is written against logical inline and block properties throughout so it survives a different writing direction. The most frequent condition this product checks is not width at all, it is whether the pointer can hover: every hover treatment sits behind that capability check, so a touch device gets the resting state and never a highlight it cannot remove. At the narrowest viewport the header collapses to the mark and a menu glyph, the documentation sidebar becomes a full-screen drawer, the pricing matrix becomes one column with a plan select, the template facets become a full-screen filter sheet with an apply control, the governance orbit becomes a vertical list of its seven labels, the console sidebar moves into a drawer with a bottom navigation, and **a data table becomes a card per row with its primary field as the heading and the rest as labelled pairs** rather than a horizontally scrolling table nobody can read. Wide decorative geometry is cropped and enlarged rather than compressed, because a graph squeezed to phone width is illegible noise while a fragment of one at readable scale still reads as a graph. Touch targets are at least the large control height on a side with untappable space between adjacent ones, nothing depends on hover or on a gesture with no single-pointer alternative, nothing locks orientation, the document reflows at four hundred percent zoom without a horizontal scrollbar, and text resizes to twice its size without losing content or function.

**Theme and forced colours.** Commit to light and design it fully; dark is a redefinition of the same eleven or so values rather than a second stylesheet, it follows the system preference by default, it is overridable, and the override is applied before first paint so there is no flash. Any mark with a fixed colour ships a mirrored pair, one hidden per theme. Under forced colours every border, focus ring and state indicator uses a system colour keyword and no meaning is carried by a background alone. Gradients interpolate in a perceptual space; interpolated naively, the blue-to-white surfaces on the marketing routes turn visibly grey in the middle.

**Accessibility floors.** One `h1` per document matching the visible title, heading levels descending without skipping, and a visually hidden heading inserted wherever the design shows none so the sequence stays intact. Real landmarks, each labelled where there is more than one of a kind. Lists are lists, so their length is announced. Everything operable by pointer is operable by keyboard with no exception anywhere, tab order follows visual order, and no positive tab index exists. Menus and listboxes take arrow keys behind a single tab stop, tabs take arrows with home and end, the data table takes arrows for cell navigation, and the command menu takes arrows and enter with escape to dismiss. Dialogs and drawers trap focus, restore it to the control that opened them, and close on escape. Focus is never moved without a user action, except into a dialog the user opened and to the first invalid field after a failed submit. Every control has an accessible name and icon-only controls are named for what they do rather than called `more`. State is programmatic - expanded, selected, checked, current, pressed, disabled, invalid, busy - and never colour alone or position alone. A deployment state chip carries its state as text as well as colour, because this is the surface people photograph into an incident channel. Live regions exist in the document before they are populated, which is the commonest reason a correctly marked notice is never announced: the search and facet counts, the copy confirmation and the build state change are polite, and the form error summary, the session expiry warning and the cap-reached notice are assertive and take focus. Nothing flashes more than three times a second. No task carries a time limit, and the session warning can be extended before it lapses.

**What must not happen.** No page where the working interface is dressed as a marketing composition.
**Internationalisation.** This product expects to be read in more than one language and its own home page says so, rendering the word for passport in five languages including two non-Latin scripts. So every layout is written against logical inline and block properties rather than left and right, the locale resolves from the reader's declared preference, then their account setting, then the request's language header, then the default, and no copy is ever assembled by concatenating fragments in code. Counts use a plural-category message rather than a letter appended in a condition. Dates, times, numbers and currency are formatted by the platform for the reader's locale, from instants and from integer minor units, rather than written out by hand. Names are one free-text field with no assumed given-and-family split and no character-set restriction beyond a length bound. No address or postcode format is assumed beyond the country field on the lead form, whose list opens with the ten most common answers before the full alphabetical list because an alphabetical-only list buries them.

**Hover is a capability, not a size.** **No behaviour depends on hover. Every hover disclosure has an equivalent** reachable by tap and by keyboard, and nothing depends on a gesture with no single-pointer alternative.

**The simulation modules, as an accessibility obligation.** Each simulation is one figure with one accessible name saying it is an illustration, its inner controls are neither focusable nor exposed as controls, its numbers sit inside the figure rather than being announced as facts about the reader's own account, and its looping motion stops under reduced motion.
 No status conveyed by colour with no word beside it. No decoration in place of content. No second accent competing with the focus colour. No simulated console that can be tabbed into.

## Technical requirements

A single-page application talking to a JSON API on the same origin. The frontend is **Preact with Vite**, built for production and served as static assets. The backend is **FastAPI** on Python 3.12 behind Uvicorn, serving the API under `/api` and serving the built frontend for every other path so a deep link into the console renders. The browser receives an application shell on first paint and fills it from the JSON API; nothing in this product is server-rendered markup.

The datastore is **PostgreSQL**, already running, reachable at `DATABASE_URL`. The identity provider is **Keycloak**, already running, reachable at `AUTH_ISSUER_URL` with `AUTH_CLIENT_ID` and `AUTH_CLIENT_SECRET`. Read every one of these from the environment and never hardcode a host, a port or a credential. Both backing services are already running and must not be downloaded, installed, compiled or started by the app.

Use only the libraries named here plus their direct dependencies. Do not introduce a second database, cache, queue, object store, identity provider or mail vendor - the only backing services available in this environment are PostgreSQL and Keycloak, and reaching for anything else is a contract violation.

Auth is an app-owned bearer token minted after the app checks the credential against Keycloak. Passwords are hashed. The token carries no claim the server does not re-resolve; the session is a server-side record and the token is an opaque reference to it.

`GET /api/health` is unauthenticated and returns `200` with a small JSON body once the app is ready, including once the database is reachable.

Authorization is one decision function with one entry point, taking the principal, the action, the resource and the request context, and returning an effect, the grant that matched, a reason and any obligations. **It runs in the request path on the server for every request, including reads.** The interface never decides; interface permission state is a hint used to hide controls and is recomputed server-side on use. A decision is cached only within a single request, never across one. A bulk operation evaluates per item and returns a per-item result.

Tenancy is enforced by the store rather than by each query. The app connects as a role with no bypass privilege, sets the acting scope once per request inside the transaction, and lets the store's own policies filter every row. **No query in the application supplies its own tenant filter.** That inverts the usual failure: a forgotten scope returns zero rows and fails loudly rather than leaking quietly.

Every value a caller sends is validated at the boundary and then trusted internally. Unknown fields on a write are refused rather than ignored, so a mistyped setting is an error rather than a setting that silently never applied. Every string has a maximum length, every collection a maximum size and every number a range. Identifiers in a path are shape-checked before any lookup. Normalisation happens once, at the boundary. Every customer-supplied string is escaped by the layer that renders it, in the context it is rendered into, and build log output is rendered as text rather than as markup because build tools emit terminal control sequences.

Background work comes in three kinds and they are genuinely different: short tasks that follow an event, long multi-step work that waits and can be compensated, and recurring work on a timetable. Every one of them declares an idempotency key and is safe to run twice, because a task that is not safe to run twice will one day run twice. **Every background task runs as an explicit principal with an explicit scope, and there is no mode that skips the authorization decision**; a task with no principal has no scope, and a task with no scope returns whatever the store happens to hand back. Retries back off exponentially with full jitter, an exhausted task lands in a dead-letter store with its full payload and error history and is individually replayable, and a task that fails at the same step three times with the same error is quarantined rather than retried to exhaustion. Every scheduled task holds a lock keyed on its name and its window so two schedulers produce one run, and records its own run with a start, an end, an outcome and item counts.

Caching is in-request memoisation plus a short-lived result cache. **Every cache key carries the acting scope and a fingerprint of the resolved grant set** - not the principal identifier, which would defeat sharing - so two principals with identical permissions share an entry and a principal whose permissions changed misses. A cache key that omits scope is a cross-tenant leak with a delay on it, which is the hardest kind to find. The app asserts at startup that every registered cache builds its key with a scope and refuses to serve if one does not. Cache writes happen after the transaction commits, never inside it. Console responses are never publicly cacheable and declare so. A miss on a hot key is single-flighted, and expiry is jittered per key.

Everywhere agreement takes time, **the lag is shown rather than hidden**. A firewall rule reports the fraction of locations already serving its bundle and is not called active until that fraction is complete. A usage figure names how far behind it is. A telemetry gap is drawn as a gap. Saying "saved" while the change is not yet in force everywhere is a false statement that eventually costs an incident.

Every response carries a request identifier, and the same identifier appears in the structured log line, in the audit record and in the error body, so one string connects what a person saw to what an operator can see. An error body carries a stable snake-case code, a human-readable message, the request identifier, optional per-field details, whether it is retryable and a documentation path. Callers branch on the code, never on the message. **No error response ever contains a stack trace, a query, an internal hostname, a table name or another tenant's identifier.**

Security posture: strict transport security, a content security policy with no unsafe inline and no unsafe evaluation, frame ancestors denied on console and identity routes, a strict cross-origin referrer policy, a permissions policy denying every capability this product does not use, content type options set and an explicit content type on every response. **No secret and no token is ever placed in an address**, because addresses reach logs, referrers, browser history and screenshots. A secret value is never returned by any read endpoint after creation: the interface shows presence, a length class and a last-updated time, and rotation is replacement. Every external call has a timeout, a bounded retry budget shared across the whole request rather than per call, and a breaker that opens on a failure ratio and half-opens on a probe. Rate limits apply per address, per principal and per organisation, with the strictest winning, and a limited response carries a retry hint.

Performance: the console shell is interactive quickly, a route transition is fast, the deployment list renders its first screen quickly and the log viewer shows its first line quickly. Long lists - deployments, logs, the audit viewer - stay smooth however long they get, and scroll at the same speed on the two-thousandth row as on the first. Reads and writes of geometry are batched and no scroll handler reads layout. Every list query has an index behind it and no surface issues a query per row. No image file is required to render any route.

Structured logs carry the request identifier, the acting scope and the principal on every line and never a personal field.
**API conventions.** One versioned surface under a path major version, one shape of address, one way of asking for the next page and one shape of error. Every collection is scoped by an explicit team or project identifier and never by an inferred default, because an inferred default is how a script writes to the wrong team. Paging is keyset: a limit plus an opaque cursor, returning the next cursor. Sorting is an allowlist of fields per collection, filtering is by declared typed parameters with no expression language on the public surface, and a field selector on a large resource draws from an allowlist. Every unsafe method accepts an idempotency key, and one is required for a create that costs money or produces a deployment. Every timestamp is an instant with an offset and no local time appears anywhere in the contract. Money is integer minor units plus a currency code, never a decimal. Compression is negotiated with the best mutually supported algorithm, and a response that echoes request content is never compressed alongside secret material.

**Validation** is declared once and used three ways - to check at the boundary, to publish the schema and to type the client - because three hand-written copies of a rule are three chances to disagree, and the day they disagree is the day something gets through. The published schema is generated from that one layer and the client is a build artefact rather than a hand-edited file; a rule change that would break the published schema fails the build with a diff, which is what makes the contract a contract.

**Injection and deserialisation.** No customer string is ever interpolated into a query, a command line, a template or a header. Queries are parameterised, and the structured query surface above is compiled from a typed tree rather than concatenated. A command runs through an argument vector, never a shell string. **There is no deserialisation of customer-controlled data into executable structures.** Environment values, project names, rule descriptions, justifications and webhook addresses are all attacker-controlled from this product's point of view and are treated that way.

**Rate limiting and abuse.** Identity submission is limited per address, per network and per account; the lead form is limited to five submissions per address per hour; the public API is limited per token, per principal, per organisation and per endpoint class; search and the assistant are limited per principal; outbound delivery is limited per endpoint with backoff; and build triggers are limited by project and team concurrency. Interactive surfaces use a bucket with a burst allowance and billing-relevant counters use a sliding window. A limited response always carries a retry hint.

**Detection.** The product watches for the shapes abuse makes: an authentication anomaly such as impossible travel, a new device on a privileged account or a burst of failures across many accounts; an authorization anomaly such as a spike of denials for one principal, which is what enumeration looks like from the inside; an audit anomaly such as a chain verification failure, an unusual export or a break-glass outside a maintenance window; and a data anomaly such as an unusual volume of reads of environment values or of the audit log. Each raises a notification that no preference can suppress.

**Cache tags and coherence.** Every cacheable response declares tags naming what it depends on, and a publish purges by tag rather than by address, because an entry appears on its own page, on an index page, in a feed and in a search result, and enumerating those addresses is how one of them goes stale. The tag vocabulary covers an entry, an entry index by kind, an entry index page, a feed by kind, the plan matrix, a documentation path, the documentation navigation, a template and the template facets. A public response is served stale-while-revalidate: the cached copy is returned at once and refreshed behind the scenes, with a stale window long enough to survive an origin outage and a hard maximum past which stale is refused and an error is served, so a permanently broken origin does not serve year-old content forever. After a mutation that principal's next read bypasses the cache for the affected keys for a short window, so they always read their own writes. **Negative results are cached briefly and separately, with a much shorter lifetime**, so a resource that is missing and then created appears quickly.

**Ordering.** Global ordering is not offered and is not claimed. Per-key ordering is, through a single-flight lock on the key, and every consumer tolerates reordering by checking a sequence number. An ordering guarantee people rely on and do not actually have is the worst kind of defect, so the contract states plainly what is ordered and what is not. Queues are partitioned by scope with weighted fair scheduling, so one organisation's burst cannot starve another's.

**Long-running work.** A build and a multi-store erasure are durable workflows rather than queue tasks. Each persists its state after every step so a restart resumes rather than restarts, declares a compensation for each step that has an external effect, **has an overall deadline and a per-step timeout**, emits progress events so a progress bar reflects real steps rather than a timer, and **is cancellable, with the cancellation propagating to the external effect**. Cancellation is cooperative first and forced after a grace period.

**Distributed-system concerns.** This is a control plane, a build record, an edge configuration and a set of external providers, and the control plane is the only writer of record. Within the control-plane database the consistency guarantee is strong and transactional. Everywhere outside it, agreement is eventual and its lag is shown: the edge configuration reports the version it is serving, telemetry states how far behind it is, and an identity provider is authoritative for identity and group membership rather than the local copy. The live channel sends **heartbeats at a fixed interval, and the client treats a missed heartbeat window as a disconnect**, reconnecting with exponential backoff and full jitter, capped, and resuming from its last offset. Server time is authoritative for every stored timestamp and client time is trusted for display only; token and assertion validation tolerates about two minutes of clock skew and no more.

**Failure principles.** Fail in one place, so a failing dependency degrades one surface and never the shell. Degrade towards reading, so reads survive longer than writes and a write is refused clearly rather than accepted and lost. Never lie about state, so "saved" means committed and, where propagation is eventual, the propagation state is shown. Preserve what the person typed across an error, a session expiry and a reload. Surface the request identifier on every failure. Under overload, shed by class in a declared order - analytics queries first, then non-critical background work, then search, then optional panels - and never the identity path and never the audit write.

**Service and recovery objectives.** Every number this product publishes has something measuring it, because a number on a public page with nothing watching it is a promise nobody is keeping. Credential minting has a latency objective, configuration propagation to every location has one, control-plane availability is aligned to the published uptime commitment, the build queue wait has one, spend estimation lag has one, first-attempt webhook delivery has one, and an audit record is durable before its response returns. Recovery objectives are stated in advance too: a control-plane region loss has a stated recovery time and a recovery point measured in seconds, a full regional data loss has a stated restore window, a corrupted configuration bundle needs no recovery because the previous version keeps serving, and **an audit chain verification failure is not repaired** - the failure is recorded, the external checkpoints establish the last verified point, and the gap is disclosed. A backup that has never been restored is a hypothesis, so restoring one is a scheduled, audited exercise with a recorded outcome.

**Dependency degradation.** Each dependency has a written degradation. The identity provider unreachable: existing sessions continue, new sign-ins for that organisation fail with a message naming the provider rather than a generic error, and owners keep a non-federated emergency route that demands a justification. The commerce provider unreachable: the console stays fully usable, plan changes are refused, and **nothing is restricted for a billing state that could not be checked**. The git provider unreachable: existing deployments are unaffected and new triggers queue and drain with their deduplication intact. The telemetry pipeline unreachable: the charts show a gap with its extent, never a zero. A notification channel failing: the other channels continue and the failure is shown in the app. The live channel failing: every surface falls back to fetching.

## Front-end specification

This section is unbudgeted and carries the visual detail the console and the public surface both draw from.

**Layering.** Five layers and dependencies point one way only: design tokens, which hold no logic; primitives, which are the button, input, select, checkbox, chip, tooltip, popover, menu, dialog, drawer, toast, table, tabs, skeleton, avatar and code block; patterns, which are the form, the data table, the faceted list, the log viewer, the chart and the empty state; features; and routes. A primitive never imports a feature.

**Tokens.** Colour is declared as hue, saturation and lightness so a dark theme is a redefinition of about eleven values rather than a second stylesheet, and an alpha ramp of ten neutral steps sits over any ground so a card border survives a coloured section. Where two steps of that alpha ramp resolve to the same value, keep both names: the duplication is a compatibility artefact and collapsing it breaks call sites that expect a stronger border from the higher number once the dark theme redefines the ramp. Five hue ramps carry status, each a ten-step scale, each publishing a bare-triple twin so a call site can compose its own alpha. Absolutes are declared in a perceptual space rather than as pure black and pure white. A contrast foreground is declared that is guaranteed to read on any accent ground.

**The mark.** A single filled triangle, drawn as geometry rather than shipped as a file. Three forms exist and they are not scaled copies of each other: a compact form for the header and the favicon, a large display form for a hero or a card, an isosceles form for the wordmark lockup, and an outlined form for use over a photographic or gradient ground, whose fill is the page ground at about sixty percent so what is underneath shows through.

**Iconography.** Every icon is drawn geometry on a sixteen-unit grid, filled with the current colour, rendered at twelve, fourteen, sixteen or twenty-four on a side. The working set covers a directional group of chevrons down, right, left, their large variants and an up-and-down select affordance, plus an arrow right; an action and status group covering download, upload, copy, external link, search, filter, settings, refresh, trash, plus, minus, close, tick, warning, information, error, clock, calendar, user, team, organisation, key, shield, lock, globe, terminal, branch, commit, deploy and rollback; control glyphs for the checkbox in its checked and indeterminate forms, the radio, the toggle and the two-rule menu; composed decorative geometry for the node field, the concentric rings of the governance orbit and the corner cross marks; and chart primitives for the axis rule, the plot grid, the area fill and the series line. Every chevron in the set shares one construction, so they sit together optically at every size, and an icon added later is built the same way rather than drawn to look similar.

**Chrome.** The header is one bar, about as tall as a large control is wide multiplied by not quite two, sitting at the top of the document and becoming sticky on scroll, with the bar, its shadow and any panel it opens each owning their own place in a declared stacking ladder rather than an ad-hoc number. Stacking is declared once as a ladder covering the drawer, the modal, the menu, the toast and the tooltip, and no product code declares a raw stacking number. The primary button is the near-black neutral ground with the contrast foreground, lightening on hover; the secondary button is page ground with a one-pixel ring drawn as a shadow so it costs no layout, and its ground lightens a step on hover. Reproduce the empty shadow stops that precede that ring, so a hover state fills one rather than making the browser re-parse the property.

**Navigation panels.** `Products` and `Resources` each open a panel below the bar, a grid of grouped links. The products groups are `Agent Stack`, `Core Platform`, `Security`, `Tools`, `Frameworks` and `SDKs`, carrying between four and eleven entries each, with a `New` chip on individual rows. A panel opens on hover after a short intent delay and on click or `Enter` with none, closes on `Escape`, on the pointer leaving both trigger and panel after a short grace, and on any navigation. Only one is open at a time and moving between triggers cross-fades rather than closing and reopening. Focus moves into the panel on keyboard activation and returns to the trigger on `Escape`, and the trigger carries its expanded state and owns the panel by identifier.

**The brand menu.** The mark opens a small menu whose items are `Copy Wordmark`, `Copy Logo`, `Download Brand Assets` and `Brand Guidelines`. The first two write the vector geometry above to the clipboard, which this build can satisfy exactly because the geometry is drawn rather than stored in a file.

**The banner slot.** A slot above the header, contributing its height to the layout so sticky offsets stay correct when it is dismissed, rendering campaigns such as `Ship 26 is coming to SF` with a `Get your ticket` action and `Meridian Agent works where you do` with an `Add to Slack` action. A dismissal is remembered per visitor and never blocks first paint.

**The footer and consent.** A consent-preferences trigger is present on every route, invisible until the consent state resolves, and it backs a real record of the categories chosen, the method, the policy version and when it was captured. Analytics and marketing scripts do not load before consent where consent is required, a global privacy signal is honoured as a refusal without a banner, and consent is asked again when the policy version changes. The footer carries `Terms` and `Privacy Policy` on the identity routes and the full sitemap elsewhere.

**Home.** The hero is two lines of display heading, `Agentic` over `Infrastructure`, two actions, `Deploy now` primary and `Talk to sales` secondary, and a right-hand list of three audience lines, `For coding agents`, `To ship apps and agents` and `Automated by agents`, each of which expands on interaction into a full sentence, with a visually hidden full sentence present for assistive technology because the split reads as three disconnected phrases otherwise. Behind it sits a soft radial field with nine circular nodes arranged in a triangle, four filled and five hollow with a hairline, over a very large blur. A logo wall follows, seven marks rendered at reduced opacity lifting to full on hover of the row, each drawn as text in the mono family with per-mark tracking rather than as an image. Then three proof bands with identical structure - a heading, a customer statistic split into a figure phrase and a trailing phrase so the number reads as the emphasis without a second size, a `Features` label and a four-item feature list, and a large panel showing a customer's own product. Then `Recently shipped` carrying three cards, one of which is a passport booklet rendering the word for passport in five languages with a coloured glow behind it. Then `Built by you, or your agents`, a `Deploy now` action, an `Onboard your agent` link and the five-group product index.

**The two very large blurs on the home page are the most expensive paint operations in this design.** Both sit on static elements, both are promoted once and never re-rasterised, and neither is ever animated. A large blur that animates is the single most reliable way to drop frames here.

**Console shell.** A sidebar naming the team sections and the current project's sections, a header carrying the team switcher, the breadcrumb, the notification bell and the command menu trigger, and a content area. The shell is rendered per principal and never publicly cached. Its navigation is prefetched on intent, its team list is held for the session and revalidated on focus, and its live channel reconnects with jittered backoff and resumes from its last offset. **Every live surface is also fetchable**: the live channel is an optimisation, and a surface that only works over it is broken for anyone behind a proxy that buffers. On reconnect the client refetches current state rather than replaying an unbounded backlog.

**The deployment board.** Columns by state, a card per deployment carrying its state chip, its commit, its author, its branch, its age and its preview address. The state chip grounds and foregrounds are: `QUEUED` on a neutral ground with a pulsing bar and no icon; `BUILDING` on a light blue ground with the thinking loader; `READY` on a light green ground with the tick; `LIVE` on the near-black neutral ground with the contrast foreground and the mark; `ERROR` on a near-white warm neutral ground with a mid, vivid red foreground; `CANCELED` on a neutral ground with the close glyph; and `SUPERSEDED` with no ground and a muted foreground. **Every chip carries its state as text as well as colour.**

**Empty and loading.** Every skeleton is sized to the content it stands in for, so layout does not shift as data arrives. Every empty state names what would be there and how to make the first one.

**Zero-asset substitution.** This is the substitution guide, and it is what removes the excuse for replacing a drawn module with a picture. Nothing in this specification requires a file to be handed over. The marks and wordmarks are drawn as geometry or set as text with tracking, the customer and partner marks are wordmarks in the mono family, photographic and screenshot panels are drawn as composed panels of the product's own primitives over a neutral ground, grain and texture are generated, and there is no audio and no three-dimensional content. Both typefaces resolve through their fallback stacks if the primary family is unavailable, and the fallback is metric-adjusted so a swap shifts glyphs rather than reflowing lines.

**Copy rules.** Sentence case for every heading except product names and proper nouns. The ampersand appears only inside compound feature names such as `Guest & Team access controls`, `SCIM & Directory Sync` and `Identity & Access`, and nowhere else. Numbers above a thousand are rendered compactly by the platform formatter rather than written out by hand, so `1.0M`, `950.8k` and `8M+` are generated and correct in every locale. Prices carry the currency symbol and a `/mo.` cadence with the full stop. Apostrophes throughout the copy deck are the straight form rather than the typographic one, which is what keeps the deck safe to search and paste. **No copy is assembled by concatenating fragments in code**: every multi-part string is one message with named placeholders, because word order is not universal. Dates and times are stored as instants and rendered in the reader's zone with their locale's format, and relative forms such as `3h`, `5h`, `12h` and `3d` come from the platform's relative formatter rather than from string building. Sorting is locale-aware. One free-text name field, with no assumption of a given and family split. Every layout tolerates copy growing by about forty percent without clipping.

## Data model

Fourteen tables. All timestamps are UTC.
The spine is four entities nested inside each other: an organisation holds teams, a team holds projects, and a project holds deployments, with domains hanging off the team and access groups, directory connections and audit records hanging off the organisation. The organisation is the contracting entity and the boundary for policy, compliance, residency and billing. The team is the collaboration unit and the first path segment. The project is a deployable unit with its own settings, environments and domains. A deployment is an immutable artefact plus its routing state.

**Every seeded account uses the password `deku-demo-pw-2026`.** It is benchmark fixture data, not a secret. Hash it as normal; the exact literal must work at login, and it must be written into `/app/USER_README.md` alongside each account so a grader can sign in.

**`organizations`** - `id`, `name`, `slug`, `plan`, `data_region`, `spend_cap_cents`, `retention_policy_id`, `created_at`, `status`. One seeded organisation, `Meridian Demo`, slug `meridian-demo`, plan `enterprise`.

**`teams`** - `id`, `organization_id`, `slug`, `name`, `plan`, `spend_cap_cents`, `default_region`, `created_at`, `status`. A team belongs to exactly one organisation and an organisation holds many, which is what lets scattered teams be absorbed under one roof without being recreated. `slug` is unique across all teams and no team may take a value from the reserved list in Core features. Two seeded teams: `northwind` on plan `pro` and `atlas` on plan `hobby`.

**`accounts`** - `id`, `email`, `name`, `password_hash`, `default_team_id`, `status`, `created_at`, `last_active_at`. `email` is unique. An account is never hard deleted; it is suspended and then anonymised, and its identifier survives so audit rows keep pointing somewhere.

**`memberships`** - `id`, `team_id`, `account_id`, `role`, `source`, `invited_by`, `joined_at`, `expires_at`, `status`. `role` is one of `developer`, `reviewer`, `finance`, `viewer`. No two rows may share a team and an account together, because a duplicate membership is a duplicate role and an ambiguous decision. A `guest` style grant always carries an expiry.

**`access_groups`** and **`access_group_members`** - a group has an `id`, `organization_id`, `name`, `description` and a `source` of `manual` or `directory`. A group sourced from the directory may not have members added by hand, because a group whose membership silently diverges from the directory is how this model rots. One seeded group, `release-reviewers`, holding `reviewer@example.com` and `reviewer2@example.com`.

**`projects`** - `id`, `team_id`, `slug`, `name`, `framework`, `default_branch`, `production_environment_id`, `data_region`, `created_at`, `status`. `slug` is unique within its team. Two seeded projects: `checkout-web` in `northwind` and `atlas-site` in `atlas`.

**`environments`** - `id`, `project_id`, `kind`, `name`, `branch_pattern`, `requires_approval`, `approval_policy_id`, `protection_mode`, `created_at`. `kind` is one of `production`, `preview`, `development` or `custom`. **A project has exactly one environment whose kind is `production`**, enforced by the store rather than by application code, because a project with two production environments has no defined promotion target. `checkout-web` seeds a `production` environment with `requires_approval` true and a `preview` environment with it false.

**`environment_variables`** - `id`, `environment_id`, `key`, `value_ciphertext`, `value_key_ref`, `kind`, `sensitive`, `created_by`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`. A key is unique within its environment, because a duplicate key resolves unpredictably at build time. Values are encrypted at rest with an organisation-scoped key, each ciphertext recording the key version that produced it so rotation is lazy re-encryption rather than a stop-the-world migration, and a value is never returned by any read.

**`deployments`** - `id`, `project_id`, `environment_id`, `source_kind`, `git_commit_sha`, `git_ref`, `git_author_login`, `state`, `ready_at`, `build_id`, `artifact_ref`, `created_by`, `created_by_kind`, `created_at`, `delivery_id`, `meta`. `state` is one of the eight values pinned in Core features. `environment_id` must belong to the same project as `project_id`; a cross-project deployment is a tenancy breach expressed as a relationship. `delivery_id` is unique where it is present, and the project, source kind and commit together are unique, which is the property that makes a replayed webhook produce one deployment rather than two. Three seeded deployments on `checkout-web`: `a1b2c3d` in `READY` on the preview environment, `9988776` currently live on production, and `5544332` in `SUPERSEDED`.

**`builds`** - `id`, `deployment_id`, `state`, `queued_at`, `started_at`, `finished_at`, `exit_code`, `log_ref`, `cache_hit`, `machine_class`, `duration_ms`.

**`aliases`** - `id`, `project_id`, `environment_id`, `deployment_id`, `host`, `path`, `assigned_at`, `assigned_by`, `previous_deployment_id`, `version`. A host and path together may name only one alias. The production alias for `checkout-web` is `checkout-web.meridian.test` and seeds pointing at `9988776`. A promotion re-points this row and records what it pointed at before, which is why rollback is re-pointing rather than rebuilding, and why it costs the same however large or old the deployment is.

**`approval_policies`** - `id`, `organization_id`, `name`, `scope_kind`, `scope_id`, `required_approvals`, `eligible_access_group_ids`, `self_approval_allowed`, `require_distinct_approvers`, `expires_after_minutes`, `freeze_windows`, `created_by`, `created_at`, `version`. The seeded row is `Production two-eyes`, version `1`, `required_approvals` `2`, eligible group `release-reviewers`, self-approval false, distinct approvers true, expiry `1440` minutes.

**`promotion_requests`** - `id`, `project_id`, `environment_id`, `deployment_id`, `requested_by`, `requested_by_kind`, `requested_at`, `policy_id`, `policy_version`, `required_approvals`, `approvals_gathered`, `state`, `justification`, `ticket_ref`, `decided_at`, `executed_at`, `execution_error`, `version`. `approvals_gathered` is **derived on read from the decision rows**, never held as a counter that is incremented in place, so it cannot drift from the decisions it counts. `version` is what makes two simultaneous approvals both land: a transition reads the version and writes with it in the predicate, and a write that finds the version moved returns a conflict instead of overwriting.

**`approval_decisions`** - `id`, `request_id`, `principal_id`, `decision`, `comment`, `decided_at`, `ip`, `request_ref`. `decision` is `approve` or `reject`. A request and a principal together may appear only once, which is what makes a second approval from the same reviewer a refusal rather than a second vote. A rejection carries a mandatory comment.

**`firewall_rules`** - `id`, `project_id`, `name`, `description`, `condition_groups`, `action`, `rate_limit`, `priority`, `enabled`, `environment_scope`, `created_by`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `hits_24h`, `last_hit_at`.

**`usage_records`** and **`usage_rollups`** - a usage record carries `id`, `scope_kind`, `scope_id`, `metric`, `quantity`, `unit`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `source`, `idempotency_key` and `recorded_at`, and `idempotency_key` is unique so an ingestion retry updates rather than duplicates. A rollup carries the scope, the metric, the period, the included quantity, the used quantity, the overage quantity, the overage in integer cents, the price version and when it was computed, and is recomputed from the records rather than accumulated.

**`spend_policies`** - `id`, `scope_kind`, `scope_id`, `hard_cap_cents`, `soft_threshold_percents`, `on_hard_cap`, `notify_access_group_ids`, `paused_at`, `paused_reason`. `on_hard_cap` is one of `notify`, `restrict` or `pause`.

**`audit_records`** - `id`, `organization_id`, `sequence`, `occurred_at`, `team_id`, `project_id`, `actor_principal_id`, `actor_principal_kind`, `actor_account_id`, `on_behalf_of_principal_id`, `action`, `resource_kind`, `resource_id`, `resource_label_at_time`, `outcome`, `reason`, `before`, `after`, `result`, `request_ref`, `ip`, `user_agent_hash`, `policy_versions_used`, `prev_hash`, `hash`. `sequence` is gapless within its organisation and a sequence value may occur only once there. **The role the application connects as holds insert rights on this table and no update or delete rights at all**, and the app verifies that at startup and refuses to serve if it is wrong. Nothing anywhere in the product removes or edits an audit row.

**`outbox_events`** - `id`, `event`, `scope_kind`, `scope_id`, `payload`, `sequence`, `created_at`, `published_at`, `attempts`. A row is written in the same transaction as the state change it describes and a relay publishes it afterwards.

**`webhook_endpoints`** and **`webhook_deliveries`** - an endpoint carries its scope, its address, the events it wants, its secrets, its status and who created it; a delivery carries the endpoint, the event, the attempt number, the response status, an excerpt of the response body, the duration and when the next attempt is due.

Two kinds of history live here and conflating them is the classic mistake. Effective-dated grants - a membership expiry, an access-group grant expiry, a break-glass window - answer who may do this **now**, and are read against the current time. The audit record answers what was true and when it was known, which is the question an auditor asks, and it is never reconstructed from current grants because current grants have forgotten every grant that was revoked.

Deletion: an organisation and a team are soft deleted and then purged after the retention window, a project is soft deleted with its aliases released immediately and its deployments kept until purge, a deployment is never removed while an alias points at it, an account is never hard deleted, and an audit record is never removed at all. No cascade crosses a tenant boundary and no cascade removes an audit row. Referential integrity is declared on every relationship rather than assumed, and every cascade is written down explicitly rather than inherited by default. The high-volume entities - deployments, builds, usage records and audit records - are partitioned by creation month so the current partition stays hot, and request-level telemetry never enters this store at all: it is aggregated, and only the aggregates are held here.

**Seed data.** One organisation `Meridian Demo`. Two teams, `northwind` on `pro` and `atlas` on `hobby`. Six accounts as listed in User roles, all with the corpus password. One access group `release-reviewers` holding both reviewers. Two projects, `checkout-web` and `atlas-site`. For `checkout-web`: a `production` environment requiring approval and a `preview` environment that does not; the `Production two-eyes` policy at version `1`; three deployments, `a1b2c3d` in `READY` on preview, `9988776` `LIVE` on production, and `5544332` `SUPERSEDED`; the production alias `checkout-web.meridian.test` pointing at `9988776`; the three firewall rules `Log request starting with /`, `Challenge user agents that look like bots` and `Deny traffic from Germany`; a spend policy with a hard cap of `100000` minor units and the action `notify`; and one webhook endpoint subscribed to `deployment.promoted`. For `atlas-site`: one `production` environment and one `READY` deployment, visible to `developer2@example.com` and to nobody in `northwind`. Seeding must be idempotent - restarting the app must not duplicate rows.

## Constraints

- One organisation with two teams. There is no organisation switcher, no organisation creation flow and no team absorption workflow.
- No real build execution. A build is a recorded state machine with recorded log lines; no customer code is compiled, containerised or run.
- No real edge network, no DNS, no certificate issuance, no second region, no regional failover and no traffic serving. A deployment's preview address is a recorded string.
- No email, no chat, no SMS and no push. A notification is an in-app record with its channel and state; nothing leaves the container.
- No payment card form, no card processor and no real invoice issuance. Billing is metering, rollups, spend policy and an invoice record.
- No third-party identity provider beyond the one already running, and no directory synchronisation endpoint. Group membership is managed in the product.
- No file uploads, no object storage and no attachments anywhere.
- No outbound network calls at runtime. A webhook delivery is attempted against the recorded endpoint and its result is recorded; nothing reaches the public internet.
- No native mobile application and no desktop application.
- No public sign-up, no password reset and no invitation acceptance flow. Accounts are seeded.
- The app must stay responsive with two hundred deployments, two thousand audit records and five hundred usage records seeded or created during a session.

## Deployment contract

- The app must be reachable at `APP_PUBLIC_URL`. The port mapping is `${APP_PUBLIC_PORT}:4173`, where `4173` is the container-internal port and `APP_PUBLIC_PORT` is what the outside world uses. Read both from the environment; never hardcode either.
- The HTTP API is served on that same origin under the `/api` prefix.
- `GET /api/health` returns `200` once the app is ready.
- The app starts from the environment image with no manual steps.
- Login credentials, or an explicit statement that there are none, are written to `/app/USER_README.md`.
- Reserved `.browser_screenshots/` and `.downloads/` directories exist at the app root, empty.
- Serve a production build behind a static or preview server, never a dev server.
- The server must keep running after this session ends and must not be a child of the shell. An ordinary background job dies with its shell, and the app will not be running when it is next opened.
- Bind `0.0.0.0`, never `127.0.0.1` or `localhost`. A loopback-only listener is unreachable from outside the container.
- The backing services named in this brief are already running and reachable at their environment variables. Do not download, install, compile or start a copy of any of them.
- Use only the providers named in this brief. No edge functions.
- No persistent volumes, no fixed container names, no custom networks.

**API shapes.** Every list endpoint returns a top-level JSON array. A successful call returns the named resource or shape; an invalid or unauthorized call is rejected as a client error, never a `5xx` and never a silent success. Bearer auth is required on everything except `GET /api/health`, `POST /api/auth/login`, the public marketing reads and `POST /api/webhooks/git`, which authenticates by signature and never by a user token.

**No mocks.** Every fact this product asserts must be real in the datastore it names. A promotion that moves an approval count on screen while the stored request is untouched is not a promotion. An audit trail held in a Python list, an in-memory `deployments` array, a hardcoded `{"state": "EXECUTED"}` the app hands back to itself, an alias that only exists in the frontend's state, or a seeded row written at request time to make a read look right are all contract violations however convincing the interface looks. PostgreSQL is the fact - the app's UI and its own reads can only reflect what lives there, never substitute for it.

## Definition of done

A developer can take a built deployment from its preview address to production: they raise a promotion request, two different eligible reviewers approve it, and traffic moves to that deployment while the one it replaced is marked superseded. Nobody can approve their own request, and a role without approval is refused by the server with the stored request left unchanged. A replayed build notification produces one deployment rather than two, and every transition is readable afterwards in an append-only audit log naming the actor and the outcome.
